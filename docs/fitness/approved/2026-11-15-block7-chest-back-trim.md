## Summary
Sebas finds Block 7 Chest-Back A too busy: 9 entries (7 slots plus the arm superset). This draft cuts both
Chest-Back days to **7 entries (6 slots)** and keeps every weekly set. Chest stays at 20, back 20, biceps 22 and
triceps 22. The incline and flat dumbbell work becomes one 30° incline press of 4 sets. The flat angle is still
covered by the Smith bench at 0°. The two rows become one barbell row, and the chest-supported row's 2 sets move
to the barbell row (+1) and the pull-up (+1). Dips and the arm superset stay. Honest note: this saves only
about 2 minutes. Session time comes from sets and rest, not from how many exercises there are. A shorter
Option B (back 16 sets a week, about 6-7 minutes shorter) is given for the PO.

The original Block 7 design called for "5-6 exercises" (`strategy.md`). Six slots puts the day back in line
with that.

## Estimated session time (weeks 2-5)

How this is estimated (Practical): about 45 s of work per set, the prescribed rest after every set (a superset
round = curl + 10 s + extension + 75 s), about 1.5 min to set up each new exercise (including one feeler set),
and a warm-up of 8 min on A days (ramp sets before the top set) or 6 min on B days.

| Day | Before | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chest-Back A | ~82 min | ~81 min | ~75 min |
| Chest-Back B | ~75 min | ~73 min | ~68 min |

Option A takes out two set-ups, but adds a set to the row and a set to the pull-up, which roughly cancels the
time gained. The day will **feel** simpler: 6 stations instead of 8, and no bench changes between the dumbbell
presses. It will not be much shorter. If time is the real problem, the levers are Option B, or the approved
fallback in the arm-emphasis doc (finisher 3 → 2 sets, about 3 min less). Do not shorten rest below 90 s on the
compound lifts.

## Decisions and evidence grades

| # | Decision | Grade | Evidence and reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep weekly sets: chest 20, back 20, biceps 22, triceps 22 | **Strong** | More weekly hard sets give more growth, with diminishing returns. 10+ weekly sets beat fewer (Schoenfeld 2017; Pelland 2026). Nothing here needs a cut in sets, so none is made in Option A. |
| 2 | 10 direct chest and 10 direct back sets per session (about 8 fractional for each arm muscle) | **Moderate** | The per-session meta-regression found growth keeps rising with sets per session, with diminishing returns. It put the point where one more set stops making a detectable difference at about 11 fractional sets per muscle per session (Remmert 2025). It is a preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) with little data at high volumes, so it is graded Moderate. 10 is under that point, and so is 8 (Option B). |
| 3 | Fewer exercises with the same sets | **Moderate / Practical** | Growth follows hard sets per muscle, not the number of exercises. Some variation helps regional growth, but more variation is not better, and excessive rotation can blunt gains (Kassiano 2022). Two chest angles plus dips, and a vertical plus a horizontal pull, still give real variation. No study tests "6 vs 8 exercises at equal sets" directly. |
| 4 | Merge 2 × incline + 2 × flat into 4 × incline dumbbell press at 30° | **Moderate** | In an RCT, incline pressing grew the upper pec more than flat pressing, and the other measured sites changed similarly (Chaves 2020: small sample, untrained men). The flat angle stays on the 0° Smith bench (A1, 4 sets), so both regions are still trained every session. Keeping incline over flat is the better-supported choice of the two. |
| 5 | Drop the "double angle" pairing in Block 7 | **Practical** (no loss expected) | Set techniques such as drop sets and pyramids give about the same growth as straight sets at equal effort (Sødal 2023; Angleri 2017). The double angle was a time-saver and a source of variety, not a growth advantage. |
| 6 | Incline press rest stays at 90 s with 4 straight sets | **Moderate** | Resting more than 60 s gives a small benefit, with nothing extra beyond about 90 s (Singer 2024). The pecs already did 4 dumbbell sets at 90 s before the merge. Expect a 1-2 rep drop on the last sets. That is normal; the engine sets the load. |
| 7 | Merge the two rows: keep the barbell row, move the chest-supported row's 2 sets to the barbell row (+1) and the pull-up (+1) | **Practical** (volume part **Strong**) | No study compares "4 + 2 sets on two rows" with "5 sets on one row". The weekly back total is what the evidence protects (#1). The barbell row stays because it is the block's heavy main lift, and heavy loads build more strength (Lopez 2021, Strong for strength). The extra sets are split so no lift goes past 5 sets, which limits rep drop-off and lower-back fatigue. The trade-off: the chest-supported row loaded the lower back less. Hence the new brace / stop cue. |
| 8 | Keep dips on the Chest-Back days (E1) | **Practical / Moderate** | Dips give a third pressing angle with the pec under stretch, plus indirect triceps work, and they carry the A day's only last-set extra (rest-pause). Moving them to the Arms days was rejected. Arms A already has 24 hard sets, and Arms B is the day he skips (his Block 6 data, approved arm-emphasis doc). That would lose chest sets in real weeks. |
| 9 | Keep the arm superset on both Chest-Back days | **Strong** (volume) / **Practical** (placement) | Unchanged from the approved arm-emphasis doc. It is the only reliable way to keep 14+ direct arm sets when Arms B is missed. Moving or dropping it would remove 2 entries but cost 6 arm sets a week. Rejected. |
| 10 | Option B (back 16) as the time-saving fallback | **Strong** that 16 is still in range / **Moderate** that 20 is slightly better | Diminishing returns (Pelland 2026) mean 16 → 20 adds a small amount of growth. Option A is the evidence-best choice. Option B is a time trade-off for the PO. |
| 11 | Week 1 and deload rules unchanged | **Practical / Moderate** (as in the approved audit; Coleman 2024; Bell 2023) | Week 1: no top set (A1 done as a back-off set), no extras (no dip rest-pause), 2-3 reps in reserve. Week 6: half the sets. The app rounds up (`Math.ceil`), so 5-set lifts become 3 sets and 4-set lifts become 2. Deload day = 15 sets (was 14). |
