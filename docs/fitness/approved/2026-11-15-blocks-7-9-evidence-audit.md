## Summary
This audit grades every key decision in Blocks 7-9 against the PO's evidence standard (2026-11-15): Strong
(meta-analyses or consistent RCTs), Moderate (single RCTs or mixed findings), Practical (no direct study). Where the
plan is not already the best-supported option, it gives the exact change at blocks.json level.

**Overall:** the plans are well built. Weekly volume, rep ranges, effort, rest and most exercise choices are
already the best-supported options or evidence-equivalent to them. The real gaps:
1. **Block 7 (starts tomorrow):** 2 changes and 1 wording fix. Swap the standing leg curl for the seated leg curl
   (Moderate), give the chest-supported row 90 s rest instead of 60 s (Moderate), and correct the "Goal" text, which
   says strength gets turned into muscle later. The evidence does not support that claim (Strong).
2. **Blocks 8-9:** hamstrings get only 6 direct sets a week and side delts 6. Both are below the ~10 weekly sets the
   dose-response research supports (Strong). Block 8 has no overhead triceps work (Moderate), and the Block 7 arm
   emphasis disappears (arms go from 22 to 12-14 sets). Block 8 week 1 stacks a second easy week on top of Block 7's
   deload.
3. **Attendance (the biggest real-world factor):** at ~4 sessions a week, the "next day in order" rule turns the
   6-day plan into a ~1.5-week cycle, so each muscle gets about two-thirds of the planned weekly sets. Evidence
   says weekly volume matters and frequency barely does once volume is equal (Strong). The current plan and a 4-day
   upper/lower plan deliver **similar** weekly volume at 4 sessions. The 4-day version (Option B below) wins on
   predictability, not on evidence: Practical.

## Method and how sets are counted
- "Direct sets" = hard sets where the muscle is the main target, weeks 2-5 (week 1 and the week 6 deload excluded).
- Indirect work (e.g. biceps in rows, triceps in presses) is counted as half a set where noted ("fractional"). The
  largest dose-response meta-regression found this method fit the data best (Pelland et al., Sports Med 2026).
- Evidence anchors used throughout:
  - **Volume:** more weekly sets give more growth, with diminishing returns. 10+ weekly sets per muscle beat fewer
    (Schoenfeld 2017; Pelland 2026). **Strong.**
  - **Frequency:** with weekly volume equal, training a muscle 1, 2 or 3+ times a week gives similar hypertrophy.
    Frequency mostly matters as a way to fit volume in (Schoenfeld 2016, 2019; Pelland 2026: frequency affects
    strength but not hypertrophy). **Strong.**
  - **Load / reps:** hypertrophy is similar from heavy (≤8RM) to light (>15RM) loads when sets are hard.
    Heavy loads give more 1RM strength (Lopez 2021; Schoenfeld 2017 low vs high load). **Strong.**
  - **Proximity to failure:** hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure (Robinson 2024). True failure
    adds nothing over stopping close to it (Refalo 2023 meta-analysis), and 1-2 RIR matched failure in trained
    lifters with less fatigue (Refalo 2024 RCT). **Strong** for "0-3 RIR"; 1-2 RIR is the efficient choice.
  - **Rest:** a small benefit to resting more than 60 s, with no extra benefit beyond ~90 s (Singer 2024 Bayesian
    meta-analysis); 3 min beat 1 min on multi-joint lifts (Schoenfeld 2016). **Moderate** (mostly untrained
    samples, wide intervals).
  - **Lengthened positions:** seated beat prone leg curl for hamstring growth (Maeo 2021). Overhead beat pushdown
    for triceps (Maeo, EJSS 2023, online 2022). Long-length partial reps can match or beat full range of motion:
    preacher curl (Pedrosa 2023), leg extension (Pedrosa 2022), calves (Kassiano 2023). In the meta-analysis this
    long-length partials signal is tentative (Wolf 2023, interval crosses zero). Exercise choice is **Moderate**;
    the partials add-on is **Moderate-to-weak**.
  - **Techniques:** drop sets give similar hypertrophy to traditional sets and save time (Sødal 2023). Rest-pause,
    myo-reps and pyramids also look equivalent, not superior (Angleri 2017 RCT). They are **Strong/Moderate for
    "equivalent"**, so they are kept as time-savers (Practical choice).
  - **Periodization:** with volume equal, periodized and non-periodized programs give the same hypertrophy.
    Periodization gives a small 1RM benefit, and undulating (A/B heavy/light days) beats linear for strength in
    trained lifters (Moesgaard 2022). **Strong.**
  - **Deloads:** a 1-week break mid-program did not change hypertrophy and slightly reduced strength gains in
    trained lifters (Coleman 2024, single RCT). Coach consensus favours flexible, need-based deloads (Bell 2023
    Delphi). **Moderate / Practical.**
  - **Exercise variation:** systematic variation helps regional growth, but excessive, random rotation can blunt
    gains (Kassiano 2022 systematic review). **Moderate.**

## Realistic attendance (6 planned, ~4 done) — clearly separate options

growth (Schoenfeld 2016, 2019; Pelland 2026). **Strong.** What matters is the number of hard sets per muscle per week
that he actually does.

problems are practical, not physiological: block weeks counted by calendar drift from the rotation, the week-6
deload arrives before the cycles are done, and leg days were skipped in Block 6 rather than done in order.

## Kept anyway — Practical (no strong evidence either way)
- **Block rotation every 6 weeks with a new method** (volume → strength → pyramid → reverse pyramid). Strong evidence
  says it neither helps nor hurts hypertrophy (Moesgaard 2022). It is kept for motivation, his preference and a
  small strength benefit.
- **Changing most exercises each block.** Kept, with the best stretch-biased lifts carried over (Kassiano 2022 warns
  only against excessive, random rotation).
- **Reverse pyramids, ascending pyramids, myo-reps, rest-pause, drop sets, half reps.** Evidence-equivalent to

- **Agonist supersets in Block 8** (press + fly, step-up + leg extension, row + pullover). Kept for time; growth data

- **Bench-angle rotation across blocks.** Regional-emphasis logic is mainstream coaching; there is no outcome trial
