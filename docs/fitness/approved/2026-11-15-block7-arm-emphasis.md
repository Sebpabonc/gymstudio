## Summary
Sebas feels his arms are not growing much. His Block 6 data agrees: direct arm work dropped through the block
(biceps 14 → 7-10 sets a week, triceps 8 → 4), mainly because Arms B is the day he misses. This draft keeps Block 7
exactly as approved and adds **one arm superset at the end of both Chest-Back days** (the days he never misses):
EZ-bar preacher curl + rope overhead triceps extension, 3 sets each. Both exercises carry over from Block 6, where
they were still progressing, so the app already has his weights for them. Direct arm work goes from 16 to 22 sets a
week, and even in a week where Arms B is skipped it stays at 14 (instead of 8). Each Chest-Back day gets about 9-10
minutes longer.

## What the data says (Block 6, sets where the muscle is the main target)

| Week | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biceps | 14 | 14 | 9 | 7 | 10 |
| Triceps | 8 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 4 |

- Block 6 only planned 8 direct triceps sets a week (4 on each arms day), and from week 3 half of that disappeared
  when Arms B was missed. Triceps make up most of the upper arm's size, so this is the biggest gap.
- Biceps got 14 when both arms days happened and 7-10 when Arms B was missed.
- The lifts themselves were moving: preacher curl 6 → 10 kg × 10, rope overhead extension 20 → 30 kg × 12 and still
  rising. Incline dumbbell curl held at 12 kg. So the exercises worked; the weekly amount was too low and depended on
  one skip-prone day.

## Decision and rationale

1. **Block 7 as written already helps.** It plans 16 direct biceps and 16 direct triceps sets a week (8 + 8 on each
   arms day), double Block 6's triceps. But it has the same weakness: if Arms B is skipped, both drop to 8.
2. **Put the extra arm work where he actually trains.** Chest-Back A and B were never missed. A short arm superset at
   the end of each turns arms into a 3-4 times a week muscle with a guaranteed floor of 14 sets each, without relying
   on Arms B. Spreading weekly sets over more sessions is a sensible way to add volume without making any one session
   very long or very hard.
3. **Choose stretch-focused moves.** The preacher curl loads the biceps hardest with the elbow nearly straight, and
   overhead triceps work trains the long head in a stretched position; both have good evidence for growth. The arms
   days already have the barbell curl and Bayesian curl (biceps), JM press and dumbbell overhead extension (triceps),
   so the finisher adds variety in angle rather than repeating the same movement.
4. **Keep what was working.** Using the Block 6 preacher curl and rope overhead extension (same ids, existing logs)
   means the app can suggest weights from real history and his progress on them continues. This is a deliberate
   exception to "change most exercises each block", for arms only.
5. **Stay inside the strength block.** The finisher uses the block's accessory rep ranges (10 on A, 12 on B), straight
   effort with no extras, and it is the only superset on those days, which matches "one superset at most per day".
   The heavy lifts at the start of the session are untouched.
6. **Protect the next day.** Each Chest-Back day is followed by an arms day in the rotation. The finisher stops 1-2
   reps short of failure so Arms A and B quality does not drop.

Rejected options: adding sets to the arms days (Arms A is already 24 hard sets and Arms B is the skipped day);
moving Arms B content to another day (changes the split and the app's day order); new exercises (not needed, and
the brief asks to reuse catalogue ids).

## Exact change, per day

Only the two Chest-Back days change. Arms A, Arms B and both Lower Body days are unchanged.

### Chest-Back A (heavy) — before
A1 Smith bench reverse pyramid 4-6-8-10 · B1 barbell row 4 × 6 · C1 30° incline DB press 2 × 8 · D1 flat DB press
2 × 8 · E1 wide pull-up 4 × 8 · F1 dip 2 × 10 · G1 30° chest-supported DB row 2 × 10

### Chest-Back A — after (new lines in bold)
A1 … G1 unchanged, then:
- **H1 `ez-bb-preacher-curls` — 3 × 10, superset, rest 10 s, angle n/a (fixed preacher pad).**
  Note: "Lower until the elbow is almost straight; never bounce at the bottom."
- **H2 `rope-low-cable-oh-tricep-extensions` — 3 × 10, superset, rest 75 s, angle n/a (cable).**
  Note: "Elbows bend fully overhead; stop 1-2 reps short for the next Arms day."

### Chest-Back B (volume) — before
A1 Smith bench 4 × 8 · B1 barbell row 4 × 8 · C1 30° incline DB press 2 × 10 · D1 flat DB press 2 × 10 · E1 wide
pull-up 4 × 10 · F1 dip 2 × 12 · G1 30° chest-supported DB row 2 × 12

### Chest-Back B — after (new lines in bold)
A1 … G1 unchanged, then:
- **H1 `ez-bb-preacher-curls` — 3 × 12, superset, rest 10 s, angle n/a.** Same note as A.
- **H2 `rope-low-cable-oh-tricep-extensions` — 3 × 12, superset, rest 75 s, angle n/a.** Same note as A.

### How it is done
Preacher curl, about 10 s to get to the cable, rope overhead extension, rest 75 s, repeat 3 times. Arms are already
warm from the rows, pull-ups and presses, so one light set of each is enough as a warm-up. Set the cable and the
preacher bench close together before starting.

### Time added
About 2.5-3 minutes per round × 3 rounds, plus 1-2 minutes to set up: **about 9-10 minutes per Chest-Back day.**

## Week rules (unchanged, applied to the new lines)
- **Week 1:** the finisher is done, at the same effort as everything else in week 1 (stop with 2-3 clean reps left).
  It is a normal exercise, not an "extra", so it is not skipped.
- **Weeks 2-5:** full plan; stop 1-2 reps short.
- **Week 6 (deload):** half the sets, same as every other exercise.

## Weekly direct sets, weeks 2-5 (muscle is the main target)

| | Block 6 plan | Block 7 before | Block 7 after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biceps, all 6 days done | 14 | 16 (Arms A 8, Arms B 8) | **22** (Arms A 8, Arms B 8, CB-A 3, CB-B 3) |
| Biceps, Arms B missed | 7 | 8 | **14** |
| Triceps, all 6 days done | 8 | 16 (Arms A 8, Arms B 8) | **22** (Arms A 8, Arms B 8, CB-A 3, CB-B 3) |
| Triceps, Arms B missed | 4 | 8 | **14** |

Per session, no muscle gets more than 8 direct sets (arms days) or 3 (Chest-Back days). Not counted above: the
biceps also work in rows and pull-ups, and the triceps in the Smith bench, dumbbell presses, dips and the overhead
press, so the real arm stimulus is higher than the direct count. 22 direct sets plus that indirect work is the upper
end of what most people recover from well; that is why the finisher stops 1-2 reps short and why there is a fallback
below.
