# Hypertrophy evidence brief for Block 6 (PT input)

- **Date:** 2026-10-05 · **Author:** UXer (research only) · **Audience:** PT, PO (Sebas)
- **Context:** intermediate lifter, 6 days/week, A/B split (chest-back, shoulders-arms, legs) ×2, goal = muscle growth.
- **Status:** research input, not approved fitness content.

## 1. Weekly sets per muscle
- More sets → more growth, with diminishing returns. Early meta-analysis: ≥10 weekly sets beat <5 ([Schoenfeld 2017](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Dose-response%20relationship%20between%20weekly%20resistance%20training%20volume%20and%20increases%20in%20muscle%20mass%20-%20A%20systematic%20review%20and%20metaanalysis.pdf)). The largest meta-regression (67 studies) found growth still rising at higher volumes but flattening, with per-session returns tapering after roughly 11 "fractional" sets ([Pelland et al. 2024](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)).
- **Practical:** 12–20 fractional sets/muscle/week; lagging muscles near the top, others 12–16; ≤ ~10 hard sets per muscle per session.
- **Indirect sets (Pelland "fractional"):** direct = 1, indirect = 0.5. Bench = 1 chest + 0.5 triceps + 0.5 front delt; row/pulldown = 1 back + 0.5 biceps + 0.5 rear delt.

## 2. Frequency
- With volume equal, frequency adds little to growth ([Pelland 2024](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)). Older data favouring 2×/week mostly reflected extra volume ([Schoenfeld 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)).
- **Practical:** the A/B split gives 2×/week; its value is spreading volume so sessions stay productive.

## 3. Rep ranges, load, proximity to failure
- Growth is similar across a wide load range (about 30–85% 1RM) **if sets are taken close to failure** ([Schoenfeld et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927075/)).
- Going all the way to failure adds only a trivial extra benefit ([Refalo 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9935748/)), but growth gets better as sets end closer to failure (lower RIR) ([Robinson et al. 2024](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/)).
- **Practical:** see rules 3–4 below.

## 4. Rest intervals
- Overall the studies overlap a lot, but limb muscles leaned toward longer rest ([Singer et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/text)).
- **Practical:** 2–3 min on compounds, 60–90 s+ on small isolation lifts (or pair them, section 6).

## 5. Exercise selection: bias the stretch
- Training at long muscle lengths, including lengthened partials, tends to match or beat full ROM for growth ([Wolf et al. 2023](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/423/880/820)). Direct trials: overhead triceps work grew the triceps about 1.4× more than pushdowns ([Maeo 2023](https://barbend.com/overhead-triceps-extensions-vs-pushdowns-muscle-growth-study)). Seated leg curls beat lying curls for the hamstrings ([Maeo 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/)). Lengthened-range calf raises grew the gastrocnemius about twice as much as full ROM ([Kassiano 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/)). Lengthened-range leg extensions grew the distal quads most ([Pedrosa 2022](https://summify.io/discover/entities/study/pedrosa-et-al-quad)).
- **Per muscle** (* = inferred from mechanics, not head-to-head tested): chest: deep DB press/fly, cable fly · lats: full-stretch pulldown/pull-up, long-reach chest-supported row* · lateral delts: cable raise from behind the body* · rear delts: reverse cable fly/pec deck* · biceps: incline or preacher curl* · triceps: overhead extension (strong) · quads: deep squat/hack squat, leg extension · hamstrings: seated leg curl (strong), RDL · glutes: deep squat, split squat, hip thrust · calves: straight-knee raise with stretch pause, bottom partials when fatigued.
- **Machine vs free weight:** similar growth; stable machines/cables make near-failure sets safer, so use them for 0–1 RIR work.

## 6. Intensity techniques
- Drop sets give similar growth to straight sets in roughly half the time ([Coleman 2022](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/46296/1/135-Article%20Text-1148-1-10-20221126.pdf)). Supersets (antagonist pairs, or upper/lower pairs) save time without hurting results. Advanced techniques are time-savers, not extra growth on their own ([Krzysztofik 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6950543/)).
- **Help:** isolation lifts, last sets, short sessions. **Hurt:** heavy compounds, when fatigue cuts later sets, or same-muscle pairings.
- **Pyramids:** no special effect; a reverse pyramid (top set, then ~10% lighter) keeps every set near failure.

## 7. Progression across 6 weeks
- Adding reps and adding load gave the same growth ([Plotkin 2022](https://peerj.com/articles/14142)).
- **Double progression:** rep range (e.g. 8–12); when **all sets** reach the top at target RIR, add the smallest step (~2.5 kg upper compounds, 5 kg lower compounds, 1–2 kg or one pin on isolation) and restart at the bottom.
- **Volume/effort:** start near the lower set target, add 1 set to priority muscles every 1–2 weeks; RIR ~3 in week 1 down to 0–1 in weeks 4–5.
- **Week 6:** a one-week break mid-programme cost no muscle, only a little strength ([Coleman 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/16777)); coaches favour fatigue-driven, not automatic, deloads ([Bell 2023](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/32417/)). **Recommendation:** week 5 is the push week. Week 6 = deload (~50% sets, 3+ RIR, same loads) **if** sleep, joints or performance slip; otherwise a normal week, deloading in Block 7 week 1.

## 8. Rules for the PT
1. Plan 12–20 fractional sets per muscle per week (indirect = 0.5); lagging muscles at the top.
2. No more than about 10 hard sets per muscle per session; split them across A and B days.
3. Compounds at 6–10 reps, 1–3 RIR; isolation at 8–15 (calves and laterals up to 20), 0–2 RIR.
4. Failure only on the last set of machine, cable or isolation lifts; never on heavy squats, deadlifts or barbell presses.
5. Rest 2–3 min on compounds, at least 60–90 s on isolation; use antagonist supersets to save time.
6. At least one stretch-biased exercise per muscle: overhead triceps, seated leg curl, incline curl, deep fly, deep-stretch calf raise, full-stretch pulldown.
7. Drop sets only on final isolation sets, max 1–2 per session.
8. Double progression with fixed load steps; log RIR.
9. Build RIR down and sets up from week 1 to week 5; week 6 is a deload or a normal week depending on fatigue.
10. Keep exercises fixed for the block so progress is measurable; swap only for pain.

*Not medical advice; pain or injury → see a professional.*
